phashlib benchmarks - python 3.11.9

1. how far does the hash move under each edit?
-----------------------------------------------
  one row per edit, applied to each of 24 scenes. The distance is bits
  that differ from the unedited original; med/min/max are over scenes.

  edit                phash          dhash          ahash         
                      med min max    med min max    med min max   
  quality
  rescale down 0.75     0.0    0    0   0.0    0    3   0.0    0    0
  rescale up 1.4        0.0    0    2   0.0    0    2   0.0    0    1
  brightness +30        0.0    0    2   0.0    0    2   0.0    0    2
  exposure x1.3         0.0    0    6   0.0    0    2   1.0    0    4
  gamma 0.8             0.0    0    4   0.0    0    2   1.0    0    4
  contrast 1.25         0.0    0    0   0.0    0    2   0.0    0    1
  noise +-12            0.0    0    6   2.0    0   14   0.0    0    2
  blur r=1              0.0    0    2   0.5    0    2   0.0    0    1
  quantise to 8 bit     0.0    0    2   0.0    0    1   0.0    0    2
  framing
  centre crop 85%       6.0    4   14   3.5    1    8   2.0    1    8
  edge crop 10%        10.0    4   16   3.0    2   10   3.0    1    8
  shift 6px            14.0   10   22  12.0    7   20   7.0    0   14
  other picture
  invert               62.0   62   62  63.0   59   64  64.0   62   64
  flip horizontal      32.0   28   34  42.0   29   59  22.0    4   52
  rotate 90            30.0   20   38  32.0   15   51  30.0   18   42

  share of edit pairs that stay inside 5 bits:
  class             phash    dhash    ahash
  quality            99.1     98.1    100.0
  framing            11.1     56.9     58.3
  quality+framing     77.1     87.8     89.6
  invert/flip/rotate      0.0      0.0      1.4
  on tone all three are good: the weakest quality share is 98.1% (dhash), and the row that separates the
  three by construction is ahash, whose threshold is the image mean - where
  the edit scales the whole image it moves a median of 1.0
  bits against 0.0 for phash and 0.0 for
  dhash; a flat brightness offset leaves all three at 0.0.
  On framing the ranking flips: phash reads the
  low-frequency spectrum of the whole frame, so cutting content out of it is
  not a small change - 11.1% of framing pairs stay
  inside the threshold against 56.9% for dhash and
  58.3% for ahash. A difference hash only sees the
  edge it was taken across and an average hash only sees the balance of the
  thumbnail, which is why they shrug the crops off better.
  Nothing here is invariant to geometry: invert sits 62 bits away at the median,
  because a negative is the opposite picture to a hash that reads
  above/below. Flip and rotate are the same story - the spectrum is indexed
  by position, so moving the content moves the hash.

  awkward frames, phash distance from the frame to its own edits:
  frame          as-is   gamma   noise  invert
  stripes            0       0       8      62
  checker            0       4      16      46
  flat-grey          0      19      32      25
  flat-black         0       0      32      32
  'as-is' is a frame against itself, so anything above 0 there would be a
  bug. On a flat field every pixel sits exactly on the threshold, so which
  side of it each of the 64 numbers falls on is decided by float rounding,
  not by the picture: 32 bits of motion out of a +-12 noise pass on a blank
  frame means the hash of a blank image is a coin flip that happens to be
  stable until you touch it. These frames are in the corpus so the tables
  above say so instead of averaging them away.

  at the threshold the literature uses (5 bits):
  hash       wrong merges quality misses framing misses closest strangers
  phash              0.0%           0.9%          88.9%              20 bits
  dhash              0.0%           1.9%          43.1%              12 bits
  ahash              0.4%           0.0%          41.7%               2 bits
  at 5 bits ahash at 0.4% merge different scenes together; phash / dhash do not. Every one of them
  loses most of the framing edits - a crop that changes what is in the
  frame is a different picture as far as these hashes are concerned, which
  is a smaller promise than 'find me the near-duplicates' and the one this
  README keeps. The FP column is also the floor of a synthetic corpus:
  smooth generated scenes are far apart (medians above 30 bits) where real
  photo sets sit much closer, so treat these as the shape of the trade-off
  and recalibrate on your own pictures before picking a number.

3. median cut versus mean cut for phash
----------------------------------------
  HashLib thresholds against the median of the 63 non-DC coefficients;
  most Python ports use the mean. Same scenes, both cuts:

  edit                median cut   mean cut
  rescale down 0.75          0.0        0.0
  rescale up 1.4             0.0        0.0
  brightness +30             0.0        0.0
  exposure x1.3              0.0        0.0
  gamma 0.8                  0.0        0.0
  contrast 1.25              0.0        0.0
  noise +-12                 0.0        0.0
  blur r=1                   0.0        0.0
  quantise to 8 bit          0.0        0.0
  centre crop 85%            6.0        7.0
  edge crop 10%             10.0        8.0
  shift 6px                 14.0       14.0
  invert                    62.0       63.0
  flip horizontal           32.0       32.0
  rotate 90                 30.0       29.0

  the cuts give the identical hash on 3 of 24 scenes;
  where they differ the gap runs from 1 to 13 of the 64 bits.
  A mean is pulled by the loudest coefficient where a median is not, so the
  frames that should split them are the ones with one loud term in the
  block - a flat field is exactly that, and the rows below check it.

  frame            cuts apart  noise moves
  scene-00                  3            0
  scene-01                  1            0
  stripes                  22            8
  checker                  15           16
  flat-grey                25           32
  flat-black                0           32
  'cuts apart' is the two hashes of the same frame; 'noise moves' is one
  cut against itself after a +-12 noise pass, so the first column is a
  choice of algorithm and the second is a choice of picture.

  related pairs inside 5 bits: median cut 222/288, mean cut 225/288.
  The cuts are not interchangeable - they disagree on the borderline pairs
  and swap a few of them each way - but on smooth synthetic scenes neither
  is a large win. Pick the median cut because a blank or near-blank frame
  has a spectrum with one loud term, where a mean moves the whole cut and a
  median does not: the 'cuts apart' column above is that effect measured,
  0-3 bits on the scenes and 15-25 on the frames with no content in them.

4. which of the 64 bits carry anything?
----------------------------------------
  28 hashes from the corpus.
  bit 0 is the DC coefficient compared against a cut that never includes
  DC, so it is set for any picture that is not pure black:
  bit 0 set on 27/28 frames; never set anywhere: none; always set: none
  bits that vary somewhere in the corpus: 64 of 64.
  the frames that leave bit 0 clear: flat-black.
  A lopsided bit is not free but it is cheap: it adds 0 to almost every
  distance and 1 to the pair that straddles it. The library's '63 usable
  bits' should be read the same way the numbers above do: bit 0 is set on
  27 of 28 frames, so it carries a fraction of a bit rather than none and
  not quite a whole one.
  fraction of frames with each bit set, 16 bits per row:
   0-15  0.96 0.61 0.21 0.43 0.43 0.46 0.61 0.39 0.43 0.32 0.50 0.64 0.36 0.46 0.39 0.36
  16-31  0.39 0.25 0.57 0.43 0.57 0.54 0.54 0.46 0.46 0.64 0.43 0.39 0.50 0.61 0.57 0.54
  32-47  0.25 0.50 0.68 0.43 0.46 0.43 0.50 0.64 0.46 0.64 0.46 0.50 0.36 0.21 0.61 0.50
  48-63  0.50 0.39 0.46 0.54 0.43 0.39 0.43 0.54 0.43 0.50 0.39 0.46 0.57 0.61 0.54 0.57
  63 of 64 bits land in the 0.2-0.8 band that actually separates images;
  the rest are almost always or almost never set and only ever move a
  distance by a bit or two at the edges of the corpus.

  distance over all 378 pairs of the whole corpus: min 20, median 32.0,
  max 44, mean 31.2 of a 64-bit hash; independent bits would average 32.
  the closest two frames are scene-00 and scene-14 at 20 bits: below that number the
  hash calls two different pictures duplicates. It is two ordinary scenes,
  not a blank one, so the assumption that only the pathological frames are
  risky is wrong on this corpus.
  the two blank frames are 32 bits apart from each other - a grey and a
  black field are not close duplicates, which is what section 1's coin
  flip predicts.

5. does the band index stay exact while skipping the scan?
-----------------------------------------------------------
  queries are stored hashes with a known number of bits flipped, so the
  exact answer is known and recall is a measurement, not a judgement.

    store  d max  bands  guarantee    recall    cands    index s    scan s vs scan
     1000      4      8       d<=7     50/50     3.1%       0.00      0.03 21.4x exact
     1000      6      8       d<=7     50/50     3.2%       0.00      0.03 20.8x exact
     5000      6      8       d<=7     50/50     3.0%       0.01      0.16 23.3x exact
    20000      6      8       d<=7     50/50     3.1%       0.03      0.63 21.2x exact
    20000      8     16      d<=15     50/50    64.5%       0.82      0.66 0.8x exact
    20000     10      8       d<=7     49/50     3.1%       0.03      0.69 20.9x OVER THE GUARANTEE

  recall is 100% wherever max_distance <= bands - 1, which is the
  pigeonhole guarantee rather than a tuned result: two hashes d bits apart
  agree in at least one of d + 1 bands, so they share a bucket and the
  index has to look at it. The row that asks for distance 10 from an
  8-band index (coverage 7) loses 1 match - that is the edge, and
  `coverage` is the field that names it instead of leaving it to a
  production incident.

  'cands' is the share of the store whose hashes get compared at all, and
  it is the whole point: it stays flat while the scan it replaces grows.
  The 16-band row pays the other half of the price - bands are forced to
  divide 64, so covering distance 8 means 16 bands of 4 bits each, and a
  4-bit bucket is 1/16 of the store. 16 buckets later the index is comparing
  64.5% of the store and lands at 0.8x the scan, so at that distance the
  index buys nothing - which is what the row says. Past a handful of bits
  apart, a linear sweep of 64-bit integers is the honest answer.

  building the 20000-hash index took 0.09 s across 2048 buckets; peak memory
  during construction was 1153 KB for a plain list of the pairs and 10188 KB
  for the index: every entry is referenced once per band, so an 8-band
  index costs about 8x the pointers of the list it replaces, and the
  buckets themselves are 1273 KB per band on average.

6. what does one image cost?
-----------------------------
  the stages of phash, one row per file the decoder has to read.

  source              decode    bytes  resize 8x8  resize 32   DCT 8x8    phash
  PGM P5 binary       0.0118    16399      0.0020     0.0039    0.0009   0.0049
  BMP 24 bit          0.0123    49206      0.0018     0.0036    0.0008   0.0052
  PGM P2 ascii        0.0180    57806      0.0018     0.0059    0.0009   0.0044

  all three files hash to the same integer as the in-memory frame
  (asserted, not assumed), so the decode step is the only place the
  formats differ: ASCII PNM is the slowest read and the largest file,
  and the cost after it is identical.

  per-image stages on a 128x128 frame (median of 40 runs each):
  stage                         ms    share
  resize to 32x32             4.09      80%
  DCT to 8x8                  0.88      17%
    of which factors          0.04       1%
  threshold, pack, residual     0.11       2%
  phash, end to end           5.08     100%
  the last row is the whole call and the rows above it are timed apart, so
  'threshold, pack, residual' is what is left once the two measured stages are
  subtracted. A negative there is timing noise between separately-timed stages
  and it means the two measured stages already account for the whole call -
  thresholding and packing 64 numbers is not where the time goes.
  The honest statement is the first row: the area-average resample is the hash.
  It reads every
  pixel of the source, while the DCT only ever touches 8 frequencies per
  axis. The cosine table is rebuilt on every call and costs 0.8%, so
  caching it would not move this number.

  whole corpus (28 frames), best of three passes:
  hash        total s   ms/image    peak KB
  phash          0.14        5.0        184
  dhash          0.06        2.2         66
  ahash          0.05        2.0         54
  ahash is the cheapest at 2.0 ms a frame, phash the dearest at 5.0: ahash
  reads an 8x8 thumbnail, dhash a 9x8 one, and phash a 32x32 block plus a
  transform. On the quality rows of section 1 all three are within a
  couple of bits of each other, so the choice between them is about
  framing behaviour and about what a false positive costs, not about speed.

7. what grouping the hashes produces
-------------------------------------
  100 images: 24 scenes each kept, rescaled, noised and centre-cropped, plus
  the 4 awkward frames. Grouped by phash with near_duplicates().

  at max_distance 5: 52 clusters, 24 groups whose three quality copies
  merged completely, 0 crops that joined their own group, 24 crops left on
  their own, 0 clusters that span two different pictures.

    scene-00/noisy, scene-00/orig, scene-00/small
    scene-01/noisy, scene-01/orig, scene-01/small
    scene-02/noisy, scene-02/orig, scene-02/small
    ...
    left on its own: scene-00/cropped, scene-01/cropped, scene-02/cropped

  `near_duplicates` is union-find, so grouping is transitive: A~B and
  B~C put all three together even when A~C is over the line. That is what
  a de-duplication pass wants, and the reason a chain of gradual edits
  reads as one group when the pairwise distances do not suggest it.

  max_distance  clusters clean groups  crops in crops alone over-merges
             3        53           23         0          24           0
             5        52           24         0          24           0
             7        48           24         4          20           0
            12        33           24        19           5           0
            20        17           11        14           0           4
  the crop columns are the ones to read against section 2: the first crop
  joins its own group at max_distance 6 and all 24 of them do by 16, while
  the two closest different scenes in this corpus are 20 bits apart. So a
  threshold of 16 buys crops at the cost of 80% of the distance budget
  before the first wrong merge - and that budget is measured on smooth
  synthetic scenes, where strangers are far apart. On a real photo set they
  sit closer, so the crop either stays a separate picture or you accept
  merges you cannot bound. That is the trade the tables above are for.
